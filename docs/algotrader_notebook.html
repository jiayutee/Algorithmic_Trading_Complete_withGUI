News / Sentiment Diffusion
Complete

Core idea

News doesn't get priced in instantly — its effect on price diffuses over time and decays, the same shape you'd see in epidemic spread or product adoption (the model borrows from Bass's 1969 diffusion model).

The model, built up

A shock is the size of a sentiment surprise. A kernel describes how much of that shock's effect is still active at time . Multiplying them gives price impact:

Multiplicative, not additive — this guarantees the effect converges to zero as and keeps the model linear in shock size (a assumption, not a law).

Worked example

Shock = +0.6, λ = 0.5/day:

Daykernel(t)impact
01.000.60
10.610.36
20.370.22
30.220.13
50.080.05

The heterogeneity problem

Not every stock, and not every news event, shares one λ. Two fixes discussed:

  • Bucket by news category (earnings / regulatory / macro) and fit one λ per bucket per stock — simple, teachable.
  • Hierarchical / partial-pooling model — every stock gets its own λ, but it's pulled toward the population average based on how much data that stock actually has (see Kyle's Lambda lesson for the full mechanics — same math, reused here).
Key insight Shock magnitude is observed directly from the news pipeline's sentiment score — it's not something to estimate. What varies by stock is sensitivity (β) to a given shock, separate from decay speed (λ).

Kyle's Lambda
Complete — sophisticated version

The three characters

An informed trader knows something the market doesn't. Noise traders trade randomly. A market maker can't tell them apart order-by-order — but sets price off net order flow, because on average, flow correlates with information.

λ is literally the market maker's "how much do I trust flow as an information signal" dial. Theoretical result (Kyle 1985):

More value uncertainty (σv) relative to noise trading (σu) → order flow is a stronger information signal → higher λ.

Empirical estimation

In practice, λ is estimated via regression on real trade data — no need to separately know σv/σu:

The sophisticated version — three corrections

1. Transaction cost decomposition. Raw price changes include bid-ask bounce (mechanical, not informational) — omitting this inflates λ. Fix: split into permanent (λ) vs. transitory (θ) components:

2. Sentiment denoising. News causes simultaneous sentiment and order-flow shocks — omitting sentiment as a control creates omitted-variable bias, inflating λ. Add γ as a control:

3. Hierarchical (varying-slope) structure. λ is the coefficient that should vary by stock (it's what we theorize differs); θ and γ stay pooled/shared — both for data efficiency and because we lack a theoretical reason to expect them to differ by stock.

Shrinkage — the weight formula

Two competing guesses for each stock's λ: its own data (uncertainty ) and the population (uncertainty ). Precision-weighted combination:

AAPL (huge history)0.75low~0.8≈0.74

σi² itself is the variance of the regression's residuals for that stock — estimated jointly with everything else via REML or MCMC, not hand-iterated.

Two connections worth remembering

Same idea, different names Partial pooling (Bayesian term) = hierarchical/multilevel model (structure) = mixed-effects model (fixed + random effects) — three vocabularies for the same shrinkage behavior.
Ridge regression connection Both shrink to fight overfitting. Ridge shrinks toward zero with a manually-tuned penalty. Hierarchical models shrink toward the population mean, with the penalty strength derived automatically from the data's own variance components — not cross-validated by hand. This is a real mathematical equivalence (mixed-model BLUPs ≈ generalized ridge solutions), not just an analogy.

Hawkes Process
In progress

Core idea

Most models of "when do events happen" (Poisson process) assume independence — one event tells you nothing about the next. Markets don't work that way: one trade or news article often triggers more soon after. Events are self-exciting.

The intensity function

  • μ — baseline event rate with nothing special happening
  • α — how big a boost each past event gives
  • β — how fast that boost decays (same exponential-decay idea as the diffusion kernel)

Worked example

μ=0.5/hr, α=2, β=1. Events at t=0, 0.5, 0.8hr. Intensity at t=1.0:

≈8× the baseline — three recent events stack their still-active decay tails.
